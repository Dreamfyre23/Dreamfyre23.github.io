Multi-Class Oral Cancer Classification Using EfficientNetV2 on Oral Photographs

An end-to-end deep learning framework for early oral cancer detection from smartphone-acquired oral photographs — combining segmentation, classification, and explainability into a single pipeline designed for low-cost screening.

Overview

Developed as part of my M.Tech in Artificial Intelligence, this project builds a complete deep learning framework for early oral cancer detection using smartphone-acquired oral photographs — aimed at enabling low-cost, accessible screening rather than requiring specialized clinical imaging equipment.

Pipeline

The framework combines three stages into a single segmentation-to-classification pipeline:

  • U-Net for oral cavity segmentation — isolating the region of interest from raw smartphone photographs and removing background noise before classification.
  • EfficientNetV2-M for multi-class disease classification across the segmented region. Selected after a comparative study of multiple deep learning architectures; EfficientNetV2-M achieved the best balance of accuracy and computational efficiency on this dataset.
  • Grad-CAM++ for Explainable AI — visualising which regions drove each prediction to support clinically meaningful interpretation. Evaluated against several XAI techniques; Grad-CAM++ produced the most accurate lesion localisation.

Dataset & training strategy

The dataset consists of smartphone-acquired oral photographs spanning multiple disease classes, sourced to reflect the accessibility constraints of real-world screening — standard clinical equipment was deliberately excluded.

Medical imaging datasets are rarely balanced across classes, and this one was no exception. Class imbalance was addressed through two complementary strategies:

  • Data augmentation — geometric and photometric transforms applied to under-represented classes to increase effective training diversity without fabricating samples.
  • Weighted focal loss — downweights easy majority-class examples and upweights harder, rarer ones during training, preventing the model from optimising purely for the dominant class.

Results & model selection

Multiple deep learning architectures were compared during the model selection phase. EfficientNetV2-M achieved the best overall classification performance among the candidates evaluated. Several XAI techniques were also assessed side by side; Grad-CAM++ was identified as the most effective for accurately localising lesion regions in oral photographs.

Full quantitative results are included in the conference paper accepted at ICCCSP 2026. Code and detailed metrics will be published alongside the paper.

Research recognition
